COVERAGE / NM-01 · FLAGSHIP

Hope's Assistant

A New York healthcare practice entered each patient visit twice, once in its records system and once in its scheduling system. Nabil specified the AI-assisted product that now makes the second entry.

1.0 The situation

The practice kept patient visits in a records system and handled billing and scheduling in a second one, and the two did not share data. Each day a staff member re-keyed the day's visits by hand: find the patient, read which doctor saw them, work out the supervising doctor, attach the billing codes, book the appointment again and check it. The practice paid for that work in staff hours and in billing mistakes, and it had not measured either. Hope's Assistant now does that job.

2.0 What NM did

He spotted the double entry, estimated what it cost, and wrote the specification for a product to replace it: read each visit → map the diagnosing doctor to the supervising doctor and billing codes → book the matching appointment. Two decisions in the specification came from him.

Staff can watch it work. Clinic staff hesitate to hand billing to software they cannot see, so the app moves a visible cursor on screen and says what it is doing while the operator watches.

The AI cannot invent a billing code. The AI may organize information, for example by turning a doctor's free-text notes into a roster of doctors and codes. A fixed set of rules then checks each AI output before the app touches the schedule.

A software engineer then built the system to that specification; Nabil kept the workflow design, the mapping logic and the client relationship.

The assistant runs on a schedule with no one at the keyboard. At the set time it opens the records system, reads the day's visits, works out each doctor and billing code, and books the day. An operator can also run it by hand in one click: pick the day and press run. A dry run shows the full day's plan before the app books anything, the way a trader checks a ticket before sending it.

4.0 Why this case comes first

This case comes first because it shows how Nabil works. He found skilled staff hours spent on re-keying, wrote a product specification with guardrails, and stayed with the project until the clinic ran it each day. A finance desk asks an analyst for the same three steps.
